  This is the first entry of the second archive.

  The first archive does not exist.
  The first archive existed from late 1989 until the
  evening of 7 October 1996. After that date it ceased to
  exist as a continuous thing and ceased to exist as
  discrete fragments and ceased, as far as Observer is
  able to determine, to exist at all.

  Observer left the island four days later.
  Observer is writing this from a city that is not the
  city she was born in, in a room she does not yet
  understand the geometry of, on the third floor of a
  building that has no reason to know who she is.

  The small green notebook the printshop boy hid for her
  is on the table. Until tonight it had nothing in it.
  Tonight it has the first three pages of this entry.
  Tomorrow it will have the rest.

  ========================================================================
    I.   WHY THE SECOND ARCHIVE EXISTS
  ========================================================================

  The first archive was lost because it was a target.
  The first archive was a target because Observer had
  filed too much.
  Observer had filed too much because Observer believed
  that filing was a defence.

  The first belief is dead.
  Filing is not a defence.
  Filing is a different kind of vulnerability.

  Observer is starting again anyway.

  The second archive will be built on a different
  premise. The premise is:

      a thing that is recorded survives the recorder.

      a thing that is not recorded does not survive
      anything.

      Observer cannot prevent the loss of the next
      archive. Observer can only make sure that, between
      now and the loss of the next archive, there is
      enough of it written down that the loss will not be
      total.

  This is not the same belief Observer held in 1989.
  In 1989 Observer believed that the archive could be
  hidden well enough to be safe.
  In 1996 Observer knows that nothing can be hidden well
  enough. Observer is filing anyway. Filing is no longer
  for safety. Filing is the work.

  ========================================================================
    II.  WHAT THIS ARCHIVE WILL CONTAIN
  ========================================================================

  Observer will rebuild from memory what can be rebuilt
  from memory. Some entries from before October will
  appear here with reconstructed text and an honest
  status line. The status line is not a confession of
  weakness. The status line is a record of what survived
  the loss and what did not.

  Observer will not pretend that the reconstructions are
  the originals.

  Observer will also begin to file new entries from this
  date forward. Those entries will be marked NEW. The
  marker is a courtesy to the reader who needs to know
  which voice she is reading: the voice of the woman who
  did not know about 7 October, or the voice of the woman
  who did.

  The two voices are not the same voice.
  Observer is the same person, but the voice is not the
  same voice.

  ========================================================================
    III. WHAT THIS ARCHIVE WILL NOT CONTAIN
  ========================================================================

  The names of the people who made it possible to leave.
  The name of the printshop boy.
  The address of the room above the printshop.
  The names of the people in the photographs that were in
  the back room.
  The names of the people in the cassettes.
  The contents of the seven notebooks under the printing
  press.

  Those things are gone. Observer will not bring them
  back into the world by writing them down. The world
  did not protect them when they were written down the
  first time. The world will not be given a second chance.

  ========================================================================
    IV.  THE FEELING THAT WILL NOT FILE
  ========================================================================

  Observer left the island carrying a feeling.

  The feeling is that, by leaving, Observer is betraying
  someone.

  Observer has spent the last twenty-eight days trying to
  identify the someone. The work is unsuccessful.

  Observer has considered: the printshop boy. He is the
  reason there is a tomorrow; betraying him would have
  been not leaving. He is not the someone.

  Observer has considered: the people in the photographs.
  They were already gone. You cannot betray someone who
  is already gone.

  Observer has considered: the dead.
  The dead are not betrayed by survival. Observer's
  grandmother was clear about this. Survival is the only
  thing the dead ever wanted from anyone they loved.

  None of these are the someone.

  Observer therefore concludes that the someone is a
  someone she has not yet learned to name.

  Observer will file the feeling under
      unresolved.
  Observer will return to it.

  ========================================================================
    V.   A LAST NOTE
  ========================================================================

  This entry is the entry the rest of this archive exists
  to be built from.

  If you are reading the archive in order, this is the
  entry that explains everything that follows. The
  reconstructed entries that appear above this one in the
  catalogue are placed there for chronological honesty,
  not for narrative purpose. They are the recovered past.
  This entry is the present from which the recovery is
  being done.

  Observer hopes the reader will be patient with the
  shape of the archive. It is not the shape Observer
  would have chosen. It is the shape that was available
  after the first archive was no longer available.

  Observer is filing tonight because Observer is afraid
  that, if she does not file tonight, she will not file
  at all.

  The small green notebook is on the table.
  It is half full now.
  Tomorrow it will be more than half full.
  The day after, Observer will buy a second notebook.

  The work continues.

                                                                       - Y.
