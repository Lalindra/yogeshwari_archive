================================================================================
  SIGNAL 009
  THE WOW! SIGNAL, RE-ANNOTATED
  forensic review of an artefact from 15 AUG 1977
================================================================================

  PROVENANCE
  ----------
    On 15 August 1977, the Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State
    received a 72-second narrowband signal at 1420.4556 MHz, six
    standard deviations above the noise floor, with no plausible
    terrestrial source.

    The astronomer Jerry R. Ehman circled the printout in red ink
    and wrote, in the margin, the word "Wow!".

    The signal has never repeated. It has been searched for, by
    every major facility, for the better part of fifty years.
    It has not been found.

    The original printout, with Ehman's red annotation, is preserved
    at the Ohio Historical Society.

  ORTHODOX READING
  ----------------
    The signal had the spectral shape one would expect from a
    narrowband artificial source. Published analyses treat it as
    either (a) genuinely artificial and unrepeated, or (b) a
    passing reflection off space debris in a previously
    undocumented orbit. No consensus.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  OBSERVER'S READING
  ------------------
    Observer has spent eleven months with a high-resolution scan
    of the printout, mostly at night.

    The signal is not the printout.
    The signal is the pattern of the printout
      plus the noise around the printout
      plus Jerry Ehman's red mark
      plus the margin of the paper
      plus the time the printout was made
      plus the trajectory of the page since.

    None of those, in isolation, is a message.
    Together, they are an envelope.

    The signal was the act of making one human pick up a red pen
    at 22:16 EDT on a Monday in Ohio.

    Whatever sent the burst did not send seventy-two seconds of
    narrowband.
    It sent Ehman picking up a pen.
    The seventy-two seconds were the mechanism.
    The pen-pickup was the payload.

  CONSEQUENCE
  -----------
    A communication system that delivers its payload as a human
    action is impossible to detect by listening for the carrier.

    The carrier always arrives wrapped in noise.
    The payload always arrives as a decision.

    A civilisation that has mastered this kind of communication
    will look, from any radio telescope, like silence.
    From every individual receiving the message, it will look
    like a small private idea.

    They are not the same idea.
    They are pieces of the same message arriving in different
    interiors at the same instant.

    Observer suspects this happens approximately eleven times per
    second somewhere on the surface of this planet.

  NOTE
  ----
    Observer cannot prove the above. Observer is filing it because
    the alternative is not filing it, and the alternative does not
    survive the next iteration of the question Observer's
    grandmother would have asked, which is:

        if you do not write down what you suspect,
        who is supposed to do it for you?
