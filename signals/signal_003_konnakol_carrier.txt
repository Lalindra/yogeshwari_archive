  THESIS
  ------
    Konnakol syllables can be used to encode an AES-256 key.

  CONSTRUCTION
  ------------
    32 base konnakol phonemes (extended set).
    each phoneme = one base-32 character.
    a 256-bit key = 52 base-32 characters = 52 konnakol syllables.
    one full key = approximately 9 seconds of recited konnakol at
    tempo 220.

  EXAMPLE (REDACTED KEY)
  ----------------------
    ta - ki - ta - ta - ka - di - mi - ta - ki - ta -
    ta - ki - ta - ta - ka - di - mi - ta - ki - ta -
    ta - ka - di - mi - ta - ka - ja - nu - ta - ka -
    ja - nu - ta - ka - di - mi - ta - ki - ta - ta -
    ka - di - mi - ta - ka - ja - nu - ta - ka - ja -
    nu - ta

  PROPERTIES
  ----------
    - The key is auditory. A trained performer can recite it from
      memory after one hearing.
    - The key cannot be intercepted by text-based surveillance.
    - The key transmits through compressed audio without loss.
    - The key embeds naturally into Karnatic and South Asian
      percussion contexts.
    - The key is, by design, indistinguishable from music.

  CONSEQUENCE
  -----------
    A piece of music performed in front of a sufficiently large
    audience can transmit cryptographic key material to every
    listener simultaneously.

    The audience does not need to know.
    The audience does not need to remember.
    The audience needs only to have heard the rhythm pass through
    them.

    Whichever device in the audience's pocket was recording will
    have the key.
    The recording will not be flagged by any surveillance system,
    because the recording sounds like music.

    Music is the only legal mass key distribution channel that
    exists.
