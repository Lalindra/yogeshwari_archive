  the broom in the courtyard arranges the gravel into seven parallel
  lines. the monk does this every morning. the gravel is the same
  gravel. the lines are not measured. the lines are not identical.

  but: any deviation from the line by a leaf, a pebble, an unintended
  human footprint, is immediately visible.

  observation:
    this is a noise floor declaration.
    by establishing what the silence looks like, the gravel exposes
    every footprint of every visitor as a signal.

    the temple has solved a problem the rest of the world does not
    know is a problem:
        how do you make a space in which every signal is legible?
    answer: first establish silence with greater rigour than the
    signal will be required to demonstrate.

  Sri Lanka does not have this practice.
  Sri Lanka does not have a baseline.
  every event is read against every other event without a reference
  level. the result is permanent disagreement on whether anything
  has happened.

  this is one of the few things Japan has correctly translated into
  national infrastructure.
  this is one of the few things every other country has refused to
  learn.

  filed under: japan, principles, noise, silence.
