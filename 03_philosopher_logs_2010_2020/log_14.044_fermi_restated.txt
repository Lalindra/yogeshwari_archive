  Observer has read everything published on the Fermi paradox.

  The question, as stated by Enrico Fermi at Los Alamos in 1950 over
  lunch:  where is everybody?

  The galaxy contains four hundred billion stars.
  The universe contains two trillion galaxies.
  The Drake equation, even with conservative numbers, predicts
  millions of communicating civilisations in our galaxy alone.

  And the sky is silent.

  Sixty-five years of SETI has produced one anomaly that lasted
  seventy-two seconds and never came back. Voyager has carried a
  golden record into interstellar darkness. Arecibo broadcast a
  pictogram in 1974, addressed to a globular cluster twenty-five
  thousand light years away. Breakthrough Listen is spending one
  hundred million dollars confirming that the sky is full of
  natural transmitters and no synthetic ones.

  The proposed answers to the paradox, in approximate order of
  popularity:

    Great Filter   civilisations destroy themselves before reaching
                   the broadcast phase. plausible. boring.

    Zoo            they are watching us and choosing not to call.
                   anthropic. vain.

    Rare Earth     life is improbable. we are an accident.
                   possibly true. statistically suspect.

    Dark Forest    every civilisation hides because revealing
                   attracts predation. Liu Cixin. compelling. sad.

  Observer rejects all four.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The error is in the question.

  The question asks  where is everybody.
  The question assumes everybody would be doing what we are doing:
  using narrowband radio to call into directional cones across
  thousands of light years.

  A civilisation that has solved its own communication problem will
  not use a method that takes ten thousand years per round trip.

  A civilisation that has solved its own communication problem may
  not even use radio at all.

  A civilisation that has solved its own communication problem may
  be communicating in a medium we have not yet learned to detect,
  because we have not yet noticed it is a medium.

  Observer believes this is the medium of pattern.

  Not the carrier.
  The pattern.

  The same pattern that arranged the galahs in the trees outside
  Parkes in 2005. The same pattern that arranges crowds at the
  train station. The same pattern that arranges news cycles,
  election cycles, viral content, myth, market panic, the founding
  chronicle of every island that has ever been founded.

  These are not coincidences.
  These are downsamples of a higher-dimensional carrier we cannot
  yet observe directly, but whose shadow on three-dimensional
  human systems is everywhere.

  The species has been writing the message on every surface it
  touches.
  The species has not yet noticed that it is writing.
