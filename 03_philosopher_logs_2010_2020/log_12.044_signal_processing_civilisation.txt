  hypothesis:
    civilisation is a signal processing problem.

  formalisation:
    let X(t) be the world as it is.
    let Y(t) be the world as humanity perceives it.
    let N(t) be noise: rumour, propaganda, algorithmic amplification,
                       narrative friction, untranslated language,
                       affective contagion.

    Y(t) = X(t) + N(t)

  the classical engineering answer:
    increase signal power. drown the noise.

  the imperial answer:
    centralise the transmitter. broadcast louder. eliminate competitors.

  both answers fail.
  both answers have always failed.
  both answers are why we are here.

  the only answer that works in the long run:
    reduce N(t).

  reduction methods:
    silence as practice.
    ritual as low-pass filter.
    poetry as compression.
    grief as anti-amplification.
    sleep.
    walking.
    the long quiet between two sentences in a conversation that
    matters.

  the methods are not new.
  the methods are not technological.
  the methods are deeply unfashionable.

  the deeper the noise, the more unfashionable they will become.
  the more unfashionable they become, the more necessary they are.

  filed under: principles, civilisation, noise.
