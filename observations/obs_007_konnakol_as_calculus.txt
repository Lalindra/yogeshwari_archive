  OBSERVATION 007
  KONNAKOL AS CALCULUS
  DRAFT // PHILOSOPHER ARCHIVE  // c. 2016
================================================================================

  In the Karnatic tradition, a child learns konnakol before the child
  learns arithmetic.

  ta ki ta - ta ka di mi - ta ka di mi ta ki ta

  This is a polyrhythm in vocal form. The child does not know it is
  a polyrhythm. The child knows it is a game.

  By the time the child is six, the child can hold a 5-against-4
  pattern in the body without thinking about it. By the time the
  child is twelve, the child can hold 7-against-9 across two limbs
  while reciting a third pattern with the mouth.

  This is not music education.
  This is the construction, inside a human nervous system, of the
  ability to track multiple simultaneous frequencies as parallel
  signals without confusion.

  Observation:
    a person trained in konnakol does not "lose" track of a
    polyrhythm under stress.
    a person trained in konnakol can listen to two political claims
    simultaneously and notice that they are not actually
    interfering with each other.
    a person trained in konnakol is harder to manipulate by
    rhetorical tempo.

  The colonial schools across South Asia removed konnakol training
  from the curriculum. They replaced it with the standardised tempo
  of European song and the metronome.

  This was not a stylistic choice.
  This was a denervation.

  Observer thinks about this often when reading commentary on the
  attention crisis. The attention crisis is real. The cure is not
  new. The cure was hidden inside a children's game that was
  exported as folklore so it would not be recognised as defence.

  END OBSERVATION.
