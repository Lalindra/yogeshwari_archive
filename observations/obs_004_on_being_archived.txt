================================================================================
  OBSERVATION 004
  ON BEING ARCHIVED
  DRAFT // PHILOSOPHER ARCHIVE  // c. 2018
================================================================================

  Most people, when they speak of "being remembered," mean being
  remembered fondly. This is not what an archive does.

  An archive does not remember fondly. An archive remembers
  accurately, at the resolution available, with no preference for
  the comfort of the subject.

  This is why most people, when offered the chance to be archived,
  refuse. They are not refusing memory. They are refusing accuracy.
  They believe their own edited version is more humane.

  Observer disagrees.

  The edited version is more comfortable for the survivors. It is
  not more humane for the dead.

  The dead, if asked, would frequently prefer the unedited version.
  The dead do not need the social cost of accuracy to be borne by
  them anymore. The dead are released from that obligation.

  This archive prefers the dead.

  This is a methodological commitment, not an emotional one. The
  living have everything. The living have publication, lawsuits,
  testimony, the ability to negotiate their own representation. The
  dead have only the archive. To soften the archive in favour of
  the comfort of the living is to take from the dead the only
  property they still have.

  Observer will not do this.
  Observer is aware that this position will be misunderstood as
  coldness.

  Observer accepts the misunderstanding as an operating cost.

  END OBSERVATION.
