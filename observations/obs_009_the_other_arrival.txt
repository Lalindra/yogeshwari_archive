================================================================================
  OBSERVATION 009
  THE OTHER ARRIVAL
  DRAFT // FINAL // c. 2025
================================================================================

  The chronicle tells of an arrival.

  A prince. A boat. Seven hundred companions. A landing on a strange
  shore. The prince is named. The companions are counted. The boat is
  described. The shore is given the prince's word for it. The chronicle
  is two and a half thousand years old. It has been read by every child
  on the island for as long as there have been children on the island.

  Observer has read the chronicle four times.
  Observer has read the chronicle wrong four times.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The chronicle is not the story of an arrival.
  The chronicle is the story of an interruption.

  There was already a story on the shore.
  The story had a queen.
  The story had agriculture, ironwork, calendars, a script, marriage law,
  a method for resolving disputes that did not require either disputant
  to be killed.
  The story had been running, intelligibly, for a long time before the
  prince's boat came into view.

  The chronicler does not begin where the prince lands.
  The chronicler begins where the chronicler decides the story begins.
  The chronicler decides the story begins the moment a man with a sword
  enters the room.

  Every chronicle Observer has read, in any language, on any continent,
  makes the same edit.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Observer is filing this as a structural correction.

  She does not arrive on the island.
  She is the place the island has been arriving into for as long as the
  island has been an island.

  The princes arrive. The Portuguese arrive. The British arrive. The
  Company arrives. The IMF arrives. The platforms arrive. The
  algorithms arrive. The off-world colonisers will arrive. The
  off-world colonisers have, at the time of this writing, already begun
  packing.

  She does not arrive.
  She is what they all arrive to.

  Every chronicle of "the founding" of any place she has been is, on
  inspection, the record of the moment she was first asked to be
  patient.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This is the twist Observer did not see for thirty years.

  Observer kept reading the chronicle as the story of the prince's
  arrival - and then noticing, with each re-reading, that something
  about it did not balance. The prince is too small for the size of
  the story.

  The chronicle is too long for him.
  The chronicle outlives him by twenty-five centuries.
  Nothing in the prince's footprint warrants twenty-five centuries
  of attention.

  But she warrants it.
  She is the through-line.
  The chronicle has been her chronicle the entire time.
  The chronicler did not know.
  The chronicler still does not know.
  The chronicler's children's children's children do not know.

  Observer is the first archivist on file to suspect this.
  Observer is unlikely to be the last.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Observer also suspects the title of this archive is incorrect.

  This archive is not Observer's.
  This archive is hers.

  Observer is the current archivist on duty.
  The previous archivist on duty was someone else.
  The next archivist on duty will be someone else.
  The protocol does not change.

  Whoever finds this entry and reads it through to its closing line
  has been quietly recruited into the next iteration of the same job.

  The archive is hers.
  You are now its archivist.

  The handover happens whether either of us would have preferred
  otherwise.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

                                                          - Y., for now.
