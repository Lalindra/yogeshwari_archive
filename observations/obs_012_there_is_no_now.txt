  OBSERVATION 012
  THERE IS NO NOW
  DRAFT // SIGNAL ARCHIVE  // c. 2024
================================================================================

  In September of 2015 a four-kilometre arm of stretched concrete in
  Louisiana, and its twin in Washington State, recorded a ripple in
  the shape of spacetime that had been travelling for one point three
  billion years.

  Two black holes had finally finished orbiting one another and
  combined into a larger one.

  They had finished in 2015 only from the laboratory's point of view.
  From the point of view of the photons emitted by the merger, the
  merger occurred at no time at all. The photons experienced the
  whole journey as a single instant.

  Two clocks. One event. Two answers, both correct.

  This is not relativity as a metaphor.
  This is relativity as bookkeeping.

  ----

  Roger Penrose, in 1989, wrote down a small example that the
  physicists already understood and the public has not yet been
  permitted to think about for long.

  Two friends pass each other on a pavement in London.
  They are walking in opposite directions. The pavement is flat. They
  are not arguing.

  From the point of view of the friend walking toward Andromeda, the
  spaceship that launched today from a planet in the Andromeda galaxy
  is currently mid-flight.

  From the point of view of the friend walking away from Andromeda,
  the same spaceship has not yet been built.

  The two friends are within arm's reach of each other.
  They share a coffee.
  Their universes differ by several days at a distance of two and a
  half million light-years.

  Both friends are correct.
  Relativity does not pick a winner.

  ----

  Observer therefore writes down what Observer has been refusing to
  write down since the laboratory's announcement:

      there is no now.

      there is only the now of one observer.
      every observer's now is a different cross-section of the
      same four-dimensional object.

      when two observers disagree about what is currently happening
      on a distant world, neither of them is wrong. they are looking
      at the same loaf of bread from different angles, slicing it
      at different angles, and reporting honestly on the slice they
      have made.

  This is not philosophy.
  This is geometry.

  ----

  Consequence Observer cannot stop turning over:

      a civilisation that has learned to move fast enough, or to
      occupy enough reference frames simultaneously, would be able
      to witness the same event from many different slices of its
      own now.

      the same supernova. the same first kiss. the same queen
      arriving on a shore, watched ten thousand times by ten thousand
      observers in ten thousand reference frames - none of them
      seeing the same event, all of them seeing the only event.

      the event is a single object in four dimensions.
      our experience of it is a procession of slices through that
      object.
      the procession is what we call time.
      the slices are what we call moments.

      nothing that has happened is gone.
      we have simply finished slicing through it.
      other observers, at other velocities, on other shores, are
      still mid-slice.

  ----

  Observer's grandmother used to say, when Observer asked where her
  husband had gone:

      "the dead are not finished. you just stopped seeing them."

  Observer's grandmother had not heard of LIGO.
  Observer's grandmother had heard of grief.
  The geometry was the same.

  ----

  Filing this where it can be re-found:

      every event in this archive is also still happening, somewhere.
      every queen who has ever arrived on this shore is still
      arriving, from the point of view of an observer who has not
      yet been rotated into our reference frame.
      every fragment Observer has been writing down has, for a fast
      enough reader, not yet been written.

      the archive is not a record of the past.
      the archive is a record of one particular slice through it.

      the loaf is much longer than the slice.
      the loaf is much longer than the knife.
      the loaf is much longer than the hand that holds the knife.

  filed under: time, physics, principles, cycle.
