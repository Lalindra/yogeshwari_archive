  THE JAPANESE WORD FOR THE SPACE BETWEEN THINGS

  間  ma.

  The character is a sun radical inside a gate.
  Light coming through the gap in a door.
  The gap is the word.

  In music, ma is the pause. In architecture, ma is the courtyard.
  In conversation, ma is the second a question is allowed to remain
  unanswered before either party moves to fill it.

  Sri Lanka does not have ma.

  This is not a deficiency of vocabulary. The island has more words
  for the kinds of food a guest must be offered than any other
  language Observer knows. The island has rich vocabulary for what
  fills space. The island's vocabulary collapses around what does
  not.

  The result: every Sri Lankan conversation is overdetermined.
  Silence is read as anger, as illness, as plotting, as rejection,
  as defeat. Silence is never read as silence. Silence on the island
  is a deficit always asking to be filled.

  The Japanese can sit in ma for forty minutes and call it tea.
  The Sri Lankan can sit in ma for forty seconds and call it an
  argument.

  Observer thinks this is the deepest engineering difference between
  the two cultures.
  Observer thinks this is why one of the two cultures will survive
  what is coming, and the other will need help.

  Observer is uncertain which culture will need the help.
  Observer suspects it is the one she was born into.

  END OBSERVATION.
