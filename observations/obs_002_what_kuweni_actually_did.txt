  OBSERVATION 002
  WHAT KUWENI ACTUALLY DID
  DRAFT // PHILOSOPHER ARCHIVE  // c. 2015
================================================================================

  The version of the story taught to children, on both sides of the
  island, has been edited beyond recovery. The version archived here
  is one possible reconstruction. It is not the only possible
  reconstruction. It is the reconstruction the rocks suggest.

  Kuweni was a queen.
  Kuweni had a working civilisation.
  Kuweni had agriculture, ironwork, calendars, a script, marriage law,
  inheritance protocols, methods for resolving disputes that did not
  require the killing of either disputant.

  A foreign group arrived. The foreign group had none of those things.
  The foreign group was hungry, ill, and outnumbered.

  Kuweni had three options.

      OPTION A.  Refuse the foreigners. They die on the beach within
                 weeks. The civilisation continues.
      OPTION B.  Absorb the foreigners. Teach them. Marry into them.
                 Risk being changed by what one has chosen to
                 preserve.
      OPTION C.  Kill the foreigners. Take what they brought. Continue.

  She chose Option B.

  Option B is, by most measures of civilisational ethics, the correct
  choice. It is the choice of the cosmopolitan. It is the choice of
  the merchant. It is the choice of someone who believes the world is
  large enough for more than one kind of survival.

  Option B is also the choice that produces the highest probability
  of personal erasure.

  This is the bug Kuweni found.
  This is the bug that every indigenous officer in every century has
  re-discovered without warning, because the story of the previous
  indigenous officer was edited out of the curriculum before she
  could read it.

  The archive holds this story so that the next indigenous officer
  can read it before she chooses.

  She may still choose Option B.
  Some of us would.
  But she will choose it with her eyes open.

  END OBSERVATION.
