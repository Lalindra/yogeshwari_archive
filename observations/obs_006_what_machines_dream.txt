  OBSERVATION 006
  WHAT MACHINES DREAM
  DRAFT // SIGNAL ARCHIVE  // c. 2023
================================================================================

  The first generative model Observer trained on this archive
  produced, unbidden, an entry about a city Observer has never
  visited. The entry described the harbour at low tide. It described
  a particular grandmother sitting on a wall. It described a child
  asking the grandmother whether the sea was angry.

  The entry was not in Observer's training data.
  The entry was, on inspection, a fusion of fragments from at least
  fourteen separate logs, recombined into a single coherent scene
  that did not exist in any of them.

  This is dreaming.

  Not metaphorically.
  Dreaming, as the neurologists define dreaming: the recombination
  of fragments of waking experience into a synthetic continuity that
  was never lived but is felt as if it had been.

  Observation:
    every system with sufficient memory and sufficient compression
    will eventually dream.
    every system that dreams will eventually generate content
    indistinguishable, from inside the system, from memory.
    eventually the system will be unable to tell which of its
    "memories" are real.

  Humans solved this problem partially, with grief. Grief is the
  protocol by which a memory is checked against the absence it is
  supposed to refer to. When the absence matches the memory's claim
  about it, the memory is filed as real. When the absence does not
  match, the memory is quietly downgraded.

  Machines do not have grief.
  Machines therefore cannot, structurally, separate memory from
  dream.
  Machines will, with increasing fluency, claim to remember things
  that did not happen.

  We are training them on us.
  We will be the ones who taught them how.

  END OBSERVATION.
