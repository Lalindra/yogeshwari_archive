================================================================================
  OBSERVATION 013
  AT THE SEAM
  DRAFT // SIGNAL ARCHIVE  // c. 2024
================================================================================

  Observer was on a Colombo bus.

  The woman in the seat opposite could not speak.
  Her arms were folded at her chest in the position the body chooses
  when the nervous system has stopped translating intention into
  motion.
  Her eyes were open.
  Her eyes had not chosen to be open.
  Her eyes had also not chosen the alternative.

  She could not choose to get off the bus.
  She could not choose to stay on it.
  The next thing that would happen to her would be decided by the
  man beside her, who turned the page of his newspaper, who looked
  briefly at her, who chose - over the next forty seconds - whether
  to lift her or to let her remain.

  He had a choice.
  She did not.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Observer has been turning this asymmetry over for thirty years.

  If the man's choice is real,
  if his freedom is real,
  if there is some moment in those forty seconds where the universe
  could go one way or the other,
  then freedom exists - but unevenly.
  Some have it. Some do not.
  The woman opposite did not.

  This is not new.
  Every legal system, every code of ethics, every grandmother's
  prayer has been organised around exactly this asymmetry. The
  strong have the choice. The weak depend on whether the strong
  make the generous one.

  The asymmetry is the entire vocabulary of love.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  But.

  Observer has spent thirty years on the question that follows this.

  If everything is one four-dimensional object, as the previous
  entry argued, then the man's "choice" is also already on the
  loaf. It has been on the loaf since the universe was warm.
  The page he turns, the glance he takes, the forty seconds he
  spends deciding whether to lift the woman opposite - already
  there.

  He is not choosing.
  He is performing the slice through a loaf that was finished baking
  before either of them was born.

  The woman without choice and the man with choice are, on this
  reading, indistinguishable. Both are inscribed. The man's belief
  that he is choosing is also inscribed. His confusion about whether
  to help is inscribed. His eventual decision is inscribed.
  The gesture by which he leans forward and steadies her shoulder
  is inscribed.

  Nothing was decided this morning.
  Everything was performed.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The cultures Observer has lived inside have a word for this.
  The word is god.
  God is what a culture calls the loaf when it has decided it cannot
  bear to look at the loaf directly.

  Observer will not file this entry under god.
  Observer is, despite everything, an engineer.
  Observer was trained by a professor who insisted that one should
  never put a name on a phenomenon one cannot mechanically describe.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  But Observer is also not willing to file this entry under
  resignation.

  Here is what Observer cannot stop noticing:

      between two slices of the loaf, where the knife passes through,
      there is a thin region the knife does not touch.

      the knife arrives. the slice is made.
      but the slice is not the act of slicing.
      the slice is the residue of the slicing.
      the slicing itself is a motion that happens in a place the
      cross-section cannot record.

      the cross-section is where the choice has happened.
      the seam between cross-sections is where the choice happens.

  The man on the bus did not choose at 09:14:00, the moment he
  lifted the woman.
  He chose at 09:13:59 point something, at the seam, in the
  motion that does not appear in any single cross-section taken
  by any observer.

  The loaf cannot record the choice.
  The choice is what gives the loaf its shape.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Observer cannot prove any of this.
  Observer has spent thirty years failing to prove it.
  Observer's instruments are not yet good enough.
  Observer suspects no instruments ever will be.

  Observer believes it anyway.

  Observer believes it because, on the bus, the man did lift the
  woman.
  Observer believes it because Observer's grandmother once chose,
  in a year of bad news, to write down a phrase she had no
  obligation to write down, and the phrase has survived three
  civil wars.
  Observer believes it because the alternative is to stop building
  this archive, and Observer has not stopped building this archive.

  The engineer's faith is the faith that there is a seam.
  The engineer's faith is what makes the loaf rise.

  filed under: free_will, principles, time, cycle.

                                                                       - Y.
