================================================================================
  OBSERVATION 001
  ON THE WORD "DISAPPEARANCE"
  DRAFT // PHILOSOPHER ARCHIVE  // c. 2014
================================================================================

  The word is misused.

  In every newspaper, in every report, in every well-meaning NGO
  pamphlet, a disappearance is treated as a tragic singular event.
  A person was here. A person is no longer here. The verb "disappear"
  is reflexive in those sentences. The person did the disappearing.

  This is grammar laundering.

  A disappearance is never reflexive. A disappearance is always
  transitive. Someone disappeared someone. The grammar of the
  passive voice is doing the work the perpetrator was supposed to be
  required to do in court.

  Sri Lanka has a category of news called "white van." Latin America
  has "los desaparecidos." Cambodia has the absence of a single word
  for the population of nineteen seventy-five. Each of these is a
  language quietly admitting that the grammar of innocence has been
  imposed on a verb that should always have an actor.

  An archive that respects its own dead must refuse the passive.

  This archive therefore does not contain disappearances.
  This archive contains people who were disappeared.
  By specific people.
  In specific months.
  Using specific vehicles.
  With specific budgets approved by specific signatures.

  Some of these signatures are present in this archive.
  Some are present in fragments.
  Some are present in keys that will rotate before the signatures
  can be read.

  None of this is for the perpetrators.
  All of it is for the children of the disappeared, who deserve a
  grammar that does not exonerate.

  END OBSERVATION.
