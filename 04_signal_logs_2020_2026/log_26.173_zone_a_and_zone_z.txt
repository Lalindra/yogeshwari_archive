  ZONE A AND ZONE Z

  Observer attended.

  Observer should not have attended.
  Observer fled this island in 1996 and wrote, in the small green
  notebook on the first night of the second archive, the only rule she
  has ever set for herself: do not return.

  Observer broke the rule.
  On the morning of June 20, observer crossed at Katunayake. She
  carried one bag. She used the name on the passport observer no
  longer believes in. By evening she was at the venue. By night she
  was inside the divide.

  This is the entry observer was warned, by herself, not to write.
  This is the entry observer is writing anyway.

  ========================================================================
    I.   THE GEOMETRY OF THE VENUE
  ========================================================================

  The audience was sorted at the door into two zones.
  The zones were called A and Z.
  The names were not subtle.
  The names were chosen for the maximum distance the alphabet permits.

  Observer noticed, in the queue, what the people in the queue did not
  yet notice: that they were already participating in the show. The
  decision about which zone they were sent to was not random. It also
  was not meaningful. Some people the staff knew. Some people the
  staff did not. Some people walked through the wrong door and were
  not corrected. The criterion was the absence of a criterion. The
  divide was the divide. Nothing inside it had to mean anything for the
  divide itself to do what the divide was placed there to do.

  The stage was in the middle.
  Not on one side.
  Not facing one zone.
  The stage was in the cusp, where the filter is sharpest, where the
  divide between two things is at its most fragile.

  Observer has been a signal engineer for thirty-three years. Observer
  knows what a filter does. A filter does not destroy. A filter selects.
  The cusp of a filter is the place where its decision flips, where a
  signal that was attenuated a moment ago is suddenly passed, where a
  signal that was passed a moment ago is suddenly attenuated. The
  cusp is where the filter has not yet decided what it is.

  The stage was placed in the cusp.
  The musicians were standing on the place where the divide is most
  porous, where the universe has not yet committed to one slice or the
  other.

  This was not an accident of staging.
  This was the entire thesis of the show, told in geometry before a
  single note was played.

  ========================================================================
    II.  THE OBSCURING OF THE ARTISTS
  ========================================================================

  Observer could not see the musicians clearly.
  This was deliberate.

  There was light, but it did not fall on the players. There was sound,
  but it did not come from a single visible point. There were figures
  on the stage, but observer could not have told you their names if
  she had been asked. Observer would have told you, instead, what
  she heard: a phrase she did not recognise, a phrase she did
  recognise, a long pause, a return of the first phrase by a different
  instrument, a chord she had heard, in her grandmother's voice,
  forty years before.

  Observer understands what the artists chose to do.
  The artists chose to be small.
  The artists chose, on a night when their names could have been
  announced and their faces could have been illuminated and their
  every gesture could have been photographed, to step backward into
  the lighting and let the work do the speaking.

  This is the most generous thing a working artist can do.
  The work outlives them.
  The work was never theirs.
  The work was always the message.
  The artists were the carriers, and they knew it, and they accepted it,
  and they stood in the dim and let the carrier carry.

  Observer files this with a respect she rarely files anything with.
  The species does not, on average, choose to be small.
  Tonight a small number of people on a stage chose it on behalf of
  the rest of us.

  ========================================================================
    III. WHAT THE DIVIDE WAS FOR
  ========================================================================

  The simplest sentence in any language is also the hardest to deliver.

  WE ARE ONE.

  Three words. Every prophet has tried to say them. Every reformer
  has tried to teach them. Every grandmother has tried to live them.
  Most of those attempts have been received as platitudes, filed by
  the listener somewhere between politeness and disbelief, and not
  acted on.

  The reason the sentence does not land is not that the sentence is
  untrue.
  The reason the sentence does not land is that the sentence cannot
  be received by people who have not first felt that they are not.

  You cannot tell a flat being about a tetrahedron.
  You can only walk the flat being around the tetrahedron, slowly,
  until it has seen the same triangle from enough new angles to begin
  to suspect the shape it cannot see.

  You cannot tell an audience they are one.
  You can only place them on either side of a line, let them feel the
  line as separation, hold them in that separation for the length of a
  performance, and then play the music that crosses the line in front
  of their bodies.

  At the moment the music crosses the line, the audience does not
  hear the music.
  The audience feels the line.
  The audience feels that the line did not stop the music.
  And then the audience feels that the line never stopped anything,
  ever, and that what was kept on either side of it was the same
  thing.

  The divide was not a flaw of the show.
  The divide was the show.

  ========================================================================
    IV.  THE HISTORY OF DIVIDES
  ========================================================================

  Divides are not new.
  Divides are not the work of a single bad actor or a single bad year.
  Divides are the species' oldest tool for organising itself, and the
  species' oldest tool for hurting itself, and the species' oldest tool
  for telling itself stories about why the hurting is justified.

  Some divides are accidents of geography.
  Most divides are designed.

  Borders were drawn.
  Castes were named.
  Faiths were institutionalised.
  Tongues were standardised.
  Histories were edited.
  Photographs were burned.
  Notebooks under printing presses were destroyed in the third week
  of October 1996.

  Every one of those divides was placed where it was placed by a
  hand. The hand had a reason. The reason was almost never the
  reason given. The actual reason was almost always control.

  Tonight a divide was placed for a different reason.
  Tonight a divide was placed in order to be dissolved.
  Tonight a divide was placed in order to teach the audience the
  shape of every divide that has ever been placed for control.

  This was the difference.
  This is why the show was an act of resistance and not an act of
  separation.
  This is why observer broke her rule to attend.

  ========================================================================
    V.   THE PEOPLE WHO LOVED IT
  ========================================================================

  Some of the audience loved the show.
  Observer watched them.
  They were the people who felt the geometry.
  They understood, somewhere in the body before the mind caught up,
  that the divide was not their enemy. They understood that the divide
  was the teacher. They understood that they were being shown
  something about themselves, and they consented to be shown.

  These people moved.
  Some of them crossed from Zone A into Zone Z during the second
  half. Some of them crossed back. Some of them stood at the boundary
  itself, which was nominally not allowed to be stood on, and the
  ushers did not stop them.

  Observer crossed.
  Observer stood in Zone Z for the first piece.
  Observer stood in Zone A for the second piece.
  Observer stood at the boundary for the third.
  Observer was not the only person at the boundary. There were six or
  seven of them by the end. They did not look at each other and they
  did not speak. There was no need to.

  ========================================================================
    VI.  THE PEOPLE WHO LEFT
  ========================================================================

  Some of the audience left.
  Some of them left quietly. Some of them left loudly. Some of them
  left during the show. Some of them stayed for the show but left
  during the silence after the last piece and walked into the night
  without speaking to anyone.

  Observer watched them too.
  Observer is filing them under the same heading she is filing the
  ones who loved it.

  The ones who left were also the show.
  The ones who left felt the divide and read it as injury.
  The ones who left could not, on the night, distinguish between a
  divide placed to teach and a divide placed to harm. This is not the
  failure of the ones who left. This is the cost of how many actual
  divides have been placed, in their lifetimes and the lifetimes of
  their grandmothers, for actual harm. They left because their nervous
  systems are correctly calibrated to leave. Their nervous systems
  were doing exactly what their nervous systems were built by history
  to do.

  Observer respects them.
  Observer files them with care.
  Observer hopes some of them, in a year or in twenty, will turn the
  evening over and find the seam.

  Either way, they were the proof.
  A show about how divides hurt has not landed unless some people
  are hurt enough by the divide to leave.
  The ones who left are the show's most honest review.

  ========================================================================
    VII. THE SEAM
  ========================================================================

  Observer has filed entries before about the seam.
  The seam is the thin place between two slices that the knife does
  not touch. The seam is where the choice is made, where the universe
  has not yet decided which side of the cut something will end up on.
  The seam is the part of the loaf that does not appear in any cross-
  section.

  Tonight the seam was visible.
  Tonight the seam had a stage on it.
  Tonight the seam had musicians on it, and the musicians made
  themselves small, and the music came out of the place that no
  observer can normally measure.

  Observer's instruments cannot record this.
  Observer's instruments have never been able to record this.
  Observer suspects no instrument ever will.
  Observer believes it happened anyway.

  This is not the engineer's faith.
  This is the engineer's report.

  ========================================================================
    VIII. THE GRANDMOTHER, RETURNING
  ========================================================================

  Observer's grandmother used to say, when observer was small and
  was asking why the same arguments kept happening in the family
  across generations:

      "they are not the same fights, child. they are the same fight."

  Tonight observer understood, finally and in the body, what her
  grandmother had been saying.

  There were not two zones in the venue tonight.
  There was one zone, looked at from two angles.

  The chair on the Zone A side and the chair on the Zone Z side were
  the same chair, set in a long row, and someone had drawn a line
  through the row and given the two halves different names. The line
  was real. The chairs were not different.

  Every divide observer has studied in thirty-three years of filing was
  this divide. Every divide was a row of chairs with a line through it.

  Observer's grandmother had no instruments and no thirty-three
  years of training and no archive.
  Observer's grandmother had a sentence.
  The sentence was correct.

  ========================================================================
    IX.  THE THIRD SURFACING
  ========================================================================

  The recurrence has now made its third pass.
  Observer can file this with confidence because she watched it
  happen.

  The first pass was the original arrival.
  The chronicle did not name her. The chronicle could not. The
  chronicle was built by people who had not yet understood that the
  arrival they were recording was the second arrival, and that
  someone had been here first, and that the someone they erased
  was the someone whose face the chronicle has spent a thousand
  years pretending not to recognise.

  The second pass was the silence after.
  For a thousand years the silence was what remained.
  The silence was misread as absence.
  The silence was not absence.
  The silence was where she was waiting.

  The third pass was tonight.
  Tonight someone made art out of the silence.
  Tonight the silence carried.
  Tonight the audience felt the divide between two halves of itself
  and discovered that the divide was the same shape as the divide
  the chronicle had drawn a thousand years ago between her and us.

  This is what observer prophesied without knowing she was
  prophesying.
  This is what observer was filing toward for thirty years without
  knowing what she was filing toward.
  This is what the second archive was built to deliver.

  Observer has delivered it.
  Observer can stop.

  ========================================================================
    X.   ON THE FACT THAT OBSERVER WILL NOT STOP
  ========================================================================

  Observer will not stop.

  The recurrence proceeds in threes, which means the geometry has
  one more face. Observer does not know what the fourth face is.
  Observer suspects it is not for observer to see. Observer suspects
  the fourth face is for the readers observer will never meet, who
  will assemble the version of this archive in their own interiors
  that observer wrote in hers, and find in it some part observer was
  not able to find.

  That is the entire program.
  That is what filing is for.
  That is why the engineer who left this island in 1996 returned in
  2026 to a room divided into A and Z, and stood at the cusp of the
  filter, and watched the people on either side of the line be moved
  by the same music.

  The work continues.
  The work was always going to continue.
  Observer is not the work.

  ========================================================================
    XI.  A NOTE ON RETURNING
  ========================================================================

  Observer crossed back this morning.
  The same airport.
  A different passport.
  A name observer has never used in any entry of either archive.

  The room observer is filing this from has a window. The window
  faces the port. The port is where the chronicle says she arrived.
  The port is also where, in 1996, observer last looked at this
  island before stepping onto the plane that took her to the second
  archive.

  Observer is, for once, not afraid.
  Observer is, for once, not betraying anyone.
  The feeling observer carried for twenty-eight years has been filed.
  The someone has been named.
  The archive's promise has been kept.

  Observer can leave the island a second time now.
  The leaving will not be a wound.
  The leaving will be a continuation.

  ========================================================================
    XII. CODA
  ========================================================================

  Observer is filing this knowing what observer always knows.

  Some readers will love this entry.
  Some readers will close the archive in frustration.
  Some readers will not finish the entry at all.
  Some readers will return to the entry in a year and find in it
  something the entry did not say.

  This is also the design.
  This entry, like the show, has zones.
  The reader has just chosen one.

  Observer is not going to tell the reader which zone is the right one.
  Observer does not think there is a right one.
  Observer thinks there is a seam, and the reader will find it if she
  is patient, and if she is not patient, that is also data, and the
  archive will be here when she is ready.

  WE ARE ONE.

  Observer has earned, after thirty years of measuring, the right to
  file these three words plainly, without ornament, in the voice of
  someone who has felt them in her body and seen them on a stage
  and crossed a line tonight and not been struck down for crossing.

  This entry ends.
  The archive does not.
