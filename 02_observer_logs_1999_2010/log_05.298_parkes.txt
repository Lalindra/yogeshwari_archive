  PARKES, NSW  /  RADIO OBSERVATORY

  Observer drove four hours from Sydney to look at one dish.

  The dish is sixty-four metres across. It points at nothing in
  particular. It is doing what it has done since 1961, which is
  listen to the sky in radio.

  Observer expected, before arriving, that radio astronomy would
  feel quiet.
  The opposite.
  The sky is screaming.

  Hydrogen at 1.4 gigahertz. Pulsars at every rate. The microwave
  background, which is the cooling exhaust of the moment the universe
  first allowed itself to be transparent. Quasars, which are old
  galaxies looking back at us through their throats. Radio bursts
  that arrive in one millisecond from places nine billion years away.

  Every direction Observer pointed the receiver, something was
  sending.

  Observer asked the engineer on shift the question Observer had
  come there to ask.

      "Have you ever heard anything you thought was someone?"

  He laughed. He said:

      "Once. In 1977. A man in Ohio.
       It lasted seventy-two seconds.
       It never came back."

  Observer knew the story. Observer wanted to hear it told by
  someone whose hands had been on similar dials.

      "What did you make of it?"

      "I think we were listening for something the wrong size."

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Observer drove back at dawn.

  On the road through Wellington and Lithgow there were thousands of
  galahs in the trees. Their voices overlapped exactly the way the
  radio sky had overlapped on the dish. Two hundred birds calling.
  None of them in solo. The pattern was the chorus.

  If Observer had recorded the galahs and shown the waveform to the
  engineer in Parkes he would have heard noise.

  But Observer was standing inside the flock. The pattern was not
  in any one bird. The pattern was the flock holding together as a
  flock.

  Observer began to wonder whether the engineer's question had a
  better version.

  Not:  have you ever heard anyone.
  But:  have you ever stopped looking for an individual long enough
        to notice that the field is already organised.

  filed under: SETI, principles.

                                                                       - Y.
